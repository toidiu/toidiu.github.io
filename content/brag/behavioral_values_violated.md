**Sample values:** Candor, Humor, Teamwork, Courage, Optimism, Thriftiness, Curiosity, Resilience, Transparency, Diversity, Responsibility, Urgency, Empathy, Rigor, Versatility

**Teamwork/Empathy**

In my experience, a high functioning team is made up of members that feel like they can make an
impact but also have the psychological safety to fail while learning. This culture was threatened at
Cloudflare, as leadership decided to hire five new junior engineers on an existing team of seven.
Onboarding is especially difficult in an async/remote team, and this is especially true for junior
engineers. The entire situation was exacerbated as three senior-tenure engineers decided to switch
teams or leave around the same time.

Having been in a similar situation before, I saw hints of stress as new engineers were expected to
start delivering in a deeply technical domain but had very little mentoring. I decided to step in
and start mentoring my teammates and ensuring they were successful.

For example, I mentored a teammate via code reviews and 1:1 meetings. Rather than flooding a PR with
nits, I limited my feedback to a few items and slowly left more specific feedback over time. I also
initiated a few ad hoc 1:1s to discuss design choices and best practices. New engineers can often be
shy, so I felt it was important for me to initiate these 1:1s. Over the next few months, the
engineer became more independent, and the code quality improved dramatically.

In another example, I just listened as a coworker vented about having to move to a new office
location. The move was tough for her due to the lack of friends and family. While I didn't actually
solve her problems, I believe listening is an important part of building trust and psychological
safety.
